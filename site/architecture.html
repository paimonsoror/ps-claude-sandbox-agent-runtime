Why two layers from one list. The proxy is where per-request, reason-bearing denials happen and where a future per-command allow list would attach. The CNI policy is the backstop with coarser granularity for everything in the pod that is not inside bubblewrap. Rendering both from the chart's single allowedDomains means a name allowed in one place and missing in the other fails loudly instead of silently widening. (ADR-0006)

Why not the obvious alternatives

Three things that look simpler and are not

A privileged pod

Strict bubblewrap works there too, but an escape is root on the node. The user namespace gives strict mode without that, and the spike shows the pod's root is host uid 2889613312.

Claude Code's own Bash sandbox, inside srt

Bubblewrap inside bubblewrap needs allowNestedUserNamespaces, and upstream says to treat the outer write denies as not holding under it. One layer around everything is stronger than two that undo each other. (ADR-0005)

An iptables firewall in the pod

The devcontainer pattern needs NET_ADMIN and NET_RAW inside the container. A policy object outside the pod needs no capability and cannot be edited by what it confines. (ADR-0006)
